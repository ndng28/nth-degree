I spent the better part of a year building a homelab before I admitted what it actually was: a portfolio.

Not a homelab. Not a "lab environment." A portfolio — the kind of thing that shows I can actually build the stuff I claim to know.

## Why a blog

The homelab touches real engineering problems: DNS routing, TLS everywhere, service isolation, backup strategies, monitoring that doesn't lie to you. Running it in production — even production of one — teaches you things no course covers.

So this is me building in public. The homelab stays behind a Tailscale mesh, but the thinking gets published here.

## What this is

The nth degree is about engineering depth — the kind you get from running production infrastructure, even if that production is one person's cloud-native homelab. Things like:

- Compliance as engineering, not paperwork
- Infrastructure that respects the data it handles
- Building things that scale to "fifty-person SaaS" even if they never will

No list of takeaways. No conclusion that restates the intro. Just posts that try to be worth reading.
